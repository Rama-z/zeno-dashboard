# Zeno English Grammar Curriculum

**Status:** Research foundation and content prototype — not a complete product syllabus  
**Subject:** English  
**Category:** Grammar  
**Target range:** A1–C1, with C2 intentionally out of scope for the first product path  
**Content location:** Repository/static files; learner-specific state belongs in the database

## 1. Scope and interpretation

This curriculum is a product-oriented synthesis, not a copy of any single publisher's syllabus.

British Council uses broad CEFR bands and a test → explanation → test lesson cycle.[1]

Its A1–A2, B1–B2, and C1 catalogues show how topic complexity and revisiting change across levels.[2][3][4]

BBC's basic and intermediate grammar guides provide useful evidence for sequencing contrasts such as present simple/present continuous, past simple/past continuous, present perfect/past simple, conditionals, passives, reported speech, and later tense reviews.[5][6]

Cambridge groups learner activities into Basic (A1–A2), Independent (B1–B2), and Proficient (C1–C2), which supports using CEFR bands as navigation and filtering rather than as a rigid one-topic-per-level rule.[7]

The CEFR itself describes communicative proficiency and grammatical accuracy; it is not a prescribed English grammar checklist. Therefore, the levels below are curriculum labels informed by CEFR-aligned resources, not claims that the Council of Europe officially assigns each listed grammar topic to that exact level.[8] English Grammar Profile research is also important because grammatical development includes form, meaning, and pragmatic appropriateness, and because a familiar form may gain new uses at later levels.[11][12]

## 2. Learning-path rules

1. **Default sequence, flexible entry:** `sequence` gives a recommended path; a future diagnostic may unlock an appropriate starting point.
2. **Explicit prerequisites:** each topic lists only the minimum concepts needed to make the lesson coherent.
3. **Meaning before labels:** lessons should first show what a structure communicates, then name and formalize it.
4. **Contrast lessons:** closely related forms should be compared after each form has been introduced independently.
5. **Mastery checkpoint:** a topic can be marked mastered only after a short objective-aligned check, not merely after opening the page.
6. **Cyclical return:** later topics intentionally revisit earlier forms in broader contexts. British Council explicitly recommends revising and practising “little and often,” and its C1 catalogue includes advanced returns to present forms and passives.[1][4]
7. **No hard-coded adaptive engine yet:** store prerequisites, objectives, review links, and stable topic IDs now; calculate recommendations later.

## 4. Cyclical review plan

Review is metadata-driven; it should not require duplicate lesson records.

- **Immediate retrieval:** after the explanation, before seeing the answer.
- **Short return:** approximately 1 day after first mastery.
- **Consolidation:** approximately 7 days later, mixed with one newer topic.
- **Interleaving:** approximately 21 days later, using contrast questions rather than identical prompts.
- **Stage review:** after each CEFR stage, mix tense, question, noun-phrase, and clause topics.
- **Path-relative checkpoints:** retrieve a topic again roughly one lesson later, three lessons later, at the end of its CEFR stage, and once more in the next stage; use these alongside time-based due dates.
- **Meaning expansion:** when a familiar form gains a new use, link back to the earlier topic (for example, present continuous → future arrangements → advanced state/action nuance).
- **Mastery recovery:** a weak review lowers confidence and schedules a nearer review; it should not erase completed lesson history.

The intervals above are product defaults, not a claim of one universally optimal schedule. A future scheduler can adjust them from learner performance.

## 5. Mastery model supported by this structure

Each topic should eventually expose:

```text
id
sequence
level
prerequisites[]
objectives[]
reviewOf[] / revisits[]
lesson content
practice items
mastery-check items
contentVersion
```

A future learner-state record can reference the stable topic `id` and store `status`, `mastery_score`, `attempt_count`, `last_reviewed_at`, and `next_review_at`. Content remains versioned in the repository.

Suggested initial states: `locked`, `available`, `in_progress`, `mastered`, `review_due`. Do not infer mastery from page views. A reasonable first rule is an 80% objective-aligned check with at least one item from each required objective, but treat that threshold as provisional. A mastery gate should combine recognition, controlled accuracy, and at least one independent-use task; it must not rely only on auto-scored multiple-choice results.

## 7. Source record and what each informed

- British Council grammar overview: CEFR-band navigation, explanation plus practice, and frequent revision.[1]
- British Council A1–A2 catalogue: foundational noun, article, quantity, comparison, and tense-contrast coverage.[2]
- British Council B1–B2 catalogue: conditionals, contrast, future forms, and other intermediate/upper-intermediate expansions.[3]
- British Council C1 catalogue: advanced passives, advanced present forms, ellipsis, inversion, emphasis, and deliberate revisiting of earlier grammar.[4]
- BBC Easy Grammar: a concrete lower-level progression from question/present forms through past, present perfect, relatives, passives, and conditionals.[5]
- BBC Medium Grammar: intermediate sequencing, mixed review, narrative tenses, reported speech, advanced future forms, participle clauses, and conditionals review.[6]
- Cambridge English learner activities: practical grouping into Basic A1–A2, Independent B1–B2, and Proficient C1–C2, plus filtering by skill and activity length.[7]
- CEFR Companion Volume: proficiency bands, grammatical accuracy as part of linguistic competence, and action-oriented learning outcomes; it did **not** supply a fixed English grammar order.[8]
- Oxford grammar archive: a cross-check that topic families such as modals, conditionals, nouns, and complex sentence building recur across learner guidance.[9]
- Oxford Practice Grammar overview: basic/intermediate/advanced content grouping and the value of tests with answer keys for checking progress.[10]
- English Grammar Profile and Cambridge's research overview: grammar development across CEFR levels should track form, meaning, use, and later expansion of familiar structures.[11][12]
